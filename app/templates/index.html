DD Financial Analysis

Interactive underwriting — set Conservative / Base / Best assumptions and see how the investment outcome changes.

DD
Select a deal report to begin

Build Conservative / Base / Best cases on one shared set of assumptions, run them deterministically, and see exactly which levers move the investment outcome.

1 Search a company above 2 Set or TRL-seed assumptions 3 Run & compare the cases

Core Assumptions

Methodology — theory & math

What it shows. Your three working hypotheses for the deal — the Conservative, Base and Best version of every assumption that drives the return — in one editable place, so you can see exactly what you're betting on and change any of it.

Theory. Underwriting under deep uncertainty: three explicit cases (Conservative / Base / Best) bound the range instead of pretending to a single point estimate. The Base case is seeded from the deal report where available (founder revenue, comps exit multiple); Conservative & Best are your downside and upside around it. Anything you don't want to model can be marked N/A and is dropped from the math entirely — never silently assumed. The outputs are only as good as these inputs; there's no hidden oracle.

Math. Revenue starts at Year-1 and grows at the CAGR, tapering toward terminal growth. Operating spend is an absolute $/yr plan (ramping ~15%/yr), independent of revenue. Exit EV = exit-revenue × multiple (EV/Revenue basis) or exit-EBITDA × multiple (EV/EBITDA basis). Ownership = entry × (1 − dilution)^rounds. MOIC = EV × ownership ÷ check. Expected MOIC = POS × MOIC + (1 − POS) × recovery.

Set each case. Tick N/A for any metric that's unknown or you don't want to model — it's dropped from the run and the sensitivity decomposition. Sensitivity and Impact populate after you run.

Scenario Comparison

Methodology — theory & math

What it shows. What you make if the deal goes poorly, as expected, or well — across every metric that matters (return, risk, exit value, runway). The spread between the Conservative and Best columns is the shape of the bet.

Theory. The same deterministic model run under each case, side by side — the core decision view. Every column is internally consistent because all of its metrics come from one run of that case's assumptions. "Expected" figures are probability-weighted; "if it reaches exit" figures assume the company survives to a liquidity event (sale / IPO), which can still be a money-losing exit.

Math. IRR = MOIC^(1/hold) − 1. DCF Value = Σ PV(FCF) + PV(terminal), discounted at the WACC. Cash to Breakeven = the deepest cumulative-FCF trough — the peak capital consumed before free cash flow turns positive (higher = worse).

On DCF Value. Treat it as a cross-check only. For early-stage deals it's dominated by the terminal value (essentially the exit-multiple bet, discounted) and is highly sensitive to the discount rate, so don't rely on it as a primary valuation — it's most meaningful for later-stage, cash-generating companies.

What Moves the Outcome

Methodology — theory & math

What it shows. A ranking of which single assumption has the most leverage on the outcome — i.e. where to spend your diligence and negotiating energy. The longest bars are the things the deal hinges on.

Theory. A one-at-a-time sensitivity probe: each assumption is flexed alone across its Conservative→Best range, with all others held at Base, and the resulting move is measured. Levers that don't enter the chosen metric are honestly shown as "no effect" rather than a fabricated zero.

Math. Two attribution modes (toggle above the bars). One-at-a-time: each bar = metric(lever at Best) − metric(lever at Conservative), others at Base; because returns compound, the bars don't sum to the total, so the leftover is shown as an explicit "Interactions" bar. Fair share (Shapley): each lever's bar is its Shapley value — its average marginal contribution across every order the levers could be added in — which fairly distributes that compounding across the levers so the bars sum to the total with no leftover. (Shapley value, cooperative game theory; the unique fair attribution — the same math behind SHAP in machine learning.)

Sensitivity Grid

Methodology — theory & math

What it shows. How the outcome responds when the two biggest levers move together, so you can spot the combinations that make or break the deal — e.g. "even at a soft 8× multiple the return still works if growth holds." Greener = better.

Theory. A two-dimensional response surface across the two assumptions a multiple-based return depends on most — Exit Multiple and Revenue CAGR. It complements the tornado, which only moves one lever at a time, by exposing how the two interact.

Math. The model is re-run at every (Exit Multiple, Revenue CAGR) cell with all other assumptions held at Base; each cell shows the resulting metric. Discount rate is deliberately not an axis — it doesn't affect a multiple-based MOIC (only the DCF).

Scenario Detail

View case:
Methodology — theory & math

What it shows. The full year-by-year financial projection behind the selected case, so you can sanity-check the story underneath the headline numbers — when revenue ramps, when the company turns cash-positive, and how deep the funding hole gets first.

Theory. Every metric above is derived from this projection; it's the actual trajectory each case implies. Switch cases with the pills above to see how the path changes.

Math. Revenue → COGS = revenue × (1 − gross margin) → Gross Profit − Operating Plan = EBITDA; FCF = Net Income + D&A − Capex − ΔNWC. Cumulative FCF gives the burn and the breakeven year; the DCF discounts FCF plus the terminal value at the WACC.
